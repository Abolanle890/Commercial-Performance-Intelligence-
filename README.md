## What Changed?
Revenue increased 5.0% in Q2, alongside an 18.2% increase in orders and a 12.7% increase in unique customers. However, AOV declined by 11.2%.  
At category level, laptop revenue increased 43.9%, while tablet revenue declined 43.3%.
Regional and channel movements also varied, highlighting areas for further investigation.

## Business Questions
This analysis answers five core business questions:
1. How did overall sales performance change from Q1 to Q2?
   - Did revenue, order volume, average order value, and customer count improve or decline?
2. What is driving the change in revenue?
   - Is revenue growth being driven by more orders, higher order value, or both?
3. Which product categories are gaining or losing momentum?
   - Which categories experienced the largest revenue movement between Q1 and Q2?
4. Which regions contributed to the change in performance?
   - Where did sales momentum increase, and which locations require further investigation?
5. How is customer purchasing behavior shifting across channels?
   - Has the balance between online and store sales changed between Q1 and Q2?

## Executive Summary
Q2 revenue reached 52M, representing a 5.0% increase compared with Q1.
The growth was accompanied by an 18.2% increase in orders and a 12.7% increase in unique customers.  
However, Average Order Value declined by 11.2%, indicating that the increase in revenue was accompanied by a lower value per order.
The category analysis shows different performance across products. Laptop revenue increased by 43.9%, while Tablet revenue declined by 43.3%, creating a clear area for management investigation.
Regional performance also varied, with Ibadan showing the strongest positive movement and Port Harcourt showing the largest negative movement in the dashboard.
The channel analysis indicates a shift in the balance between store and online revenue, which may warrant further investigation into changes in customer purchasing behavior.

## Key Findings
1. Revenue grew, but order value declined
   
| KPI | Q2 | Change vs Q1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Orders | 240 | **+18.2%** |
| Revenue | 52M | **+5.0%** |
| Average Order Value | 422K | **-11.2%** |
| Unique Customers | 71 | **+12.7%** |

The important business signal is not simply that revenue increased.
Revenue grew more slowly than order volume, while AOV declined.

That creates a useful management question:
- Are we generating more revenue primarily by increasing transaction volume rather than increasing the value of each transaction?

2. Product performance is uneven
   
- Laptop revenue: +43.9%
- Tablet revenue: -43.3%

This suggests that overall revenue growth is masking significant differences at the product-category level.

Management may therefore need to investigate:
- What is driving laptop growth?
- What changed in tablet sales?
- Are customers shifting demand between categories?
- Are pricing, promotions, availability, or channel performance contributing to the movement?

3. Regional performance requires a closer look
   
The regional movement analysis shows meaningful differences across locations.
Ibadan recorded the strongest positive movement, while Port Harcourt recorded the largest decline.

The next analytical step would be to investigate whether the regional differences are associated with:
- order volume
- customer acquisition
- product mix
- channel mix
- or average order value

## Management Questions

Based on the Q1–Q2 results, the immediate areas for investigation are:
1. AOV decline
Orders increased 18.2%, but AOV fell 11.2%. Management should understand what changed in the composition or value of individual orders.
2. Tablet decline
Tablet revenue fell 43.3% and represents a significant category-level movement requiring investigation.
3. Laptop growth
Laptop revenue increased 43.9%. Understanding what is behind this growth could help explain changes in the product mix.
4. Regional variation
The difference between positive movement in Ibadan and negative movement in Port Harcourt warrants a deeper regional analysis.
5. Channel movement
The shift between online and store revenue should be examined alongside product, customer, and regional performance.
